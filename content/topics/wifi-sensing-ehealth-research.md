Estimates of welfare technology spending in 2049 are projected to reach 8 billion NOKs per year. Yet reusing existing equipment in patient homes might make health expenditures much more sustainable. In particular, WiFi sensing could be used as a passive way to capture the activity and daily functioning of elderly patients at home, without asking them to wear or operate anything. The idea is that such data could support the goal of a private conversation between patient and General Practitioners and give patients their own insight into how they are doing. 

The project might focus on sensing based on beamforming feedback information (BFI), and it might include building a small experimental set-up, testing what activity and presence information can be extracted from BFI, and assessing how reliable it is in realistic home conditions.
