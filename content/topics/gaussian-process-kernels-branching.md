When cells of a growing organism divide, daughter cells inherit similar gene expression profiles from their mother cell and stay in close physical proximity. Qualitatively it has been argued that this principle could provide an efficient encoding of the spatial wiring of the brain, but a quantitative analysis of the hierarchical covariance structure of gene expression profiles generated by such spatial branching processes has not yet been performed. The aim of this project is to derive, analytically and through simulation, 2D and 3D hierarchical spatial distance kernels that result from different choices for the cellular branching and stochastic gene expression models, and use Gaussian process models based on these kernels to learn kinetic parameters and causal interactions from spatial gene expression data from the brain and other organs.
